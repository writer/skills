# CPG & Product Claims (US / EU / UK)

Load this for PDPs, product ads, packaging-adjacent copy and any CPG product claim. It is a risk checklist, not legal advice. Route anything flagged here to the brand's regulatory or legal team, and use only the approved wording.

## 1. Classify every claim before judging it
| Claim type | Example | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **Drug / disease** (diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, prevent) | "prevents colds", "treats acne" (unless it's a drug) | **Critical** on any non-drug product (food, supplement, cosmetic) |
| **Structure / function** (supplements, foods) | "supports immune health" | Supplements: the exact DSHEA disclaimer is required, and FDA must be notified no later than 30 days after first marketing (21 CFR 101.93) |
| **Health claim** (disease-risk reduction) | "calcium may reduce the risk of osteoporosis" | Only FDA-authorized or qualified health claims, in the prescribed wording |
| **Nutrient content** | "good source of fiber", "low fat" | Must meet defined thresholds (below) |
| **Cosmetic vs. drug** | "reduces the *appearance* of wrinkles" vs. "repairs collagen" | Function language ("repairs", "regrows", "heals") makes it a drug claim |
| **Antimicrobial** | "kills 99.9% of germs", "antibacterial", "disinfects", "sanitizes" | **On surfaces and objects:** a pesticidal claim that needs EPA FIFRA registration; the treated-article exemption covers only protecting the article itself. **On skin or body** (hand soap, sanitizer, skin wipes): an FDA OTC drug claim under the antiseptic monographs. **Critical** on unregistered or non-drug products either way |
| **Environmental** | "recyclable", "carbon neutral", "eco-friendly" | §3 below |
| **Comparative / superiority** | "#1", "2x longer than Brand X" | Head-to-head substantiation |
| **Endorsement / testimonial / review** | | `claims-and-compliance.md` §3 |

Judge claims by their **net impression**, including implied claims. A plant leaf beside "pure" can imply "natural" or "safe".

## 2. US specifics
- **DSHEA disclaimer** (exact wording, next to the claim or linked by a symbol):
  - "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."
  - Use "These statements have…" when there are several claims.
- **Nutrient content thresholds:**
  - "Good source" means 10–19% of the Daily Value per reference amount (21 CFR 101.54).
  - "High", "rich in" or "excellent source" means 20% DV or more.
  - "Gluten-free" means under 20 ppm gluten (21 CFR 101.91).
  - "Healthy" follows FDA's updated definition (21 CFR 101.65(d), effective 28 April 2025). Products may use the new criteria now and must comply by 25 February 2028. Confirm which definition the label was approved under.
- **"Natural":** FDA has no formal definition, so treat it as high risk and use only approved wording. Exception: USDA FSIS does define "natural" for meat and poultry (minimal processing, no artificial ingredients).
- **"Clinically proven" or "clinically tested":** needs competent and reliable scientific evidence for *that* claim, formulation and dose, usually well-controlled human studies. Never invent study details.
- **"#1", "best-selling", "most recommended":** state the data source, period, category definition and channel next to the claim. "Doctor recommended" needs survey evidence.
- **Taste and preference claims:** need properly designed consumer testing.
- **Downgrading a claim:** a drug claim can sometimes become a structure/function claim plus the disclaimer. Only do this with wording from the approved claims list; never write new regulatory wording.
- **Comparative ads:** they carry Lanham Act §43(a) exposure (competitor lawsuits) and NAD challenge risk, in addition to FTC.
- **California Prop 65:** warnings where an exposure applies; the brand decides.
- **Retail media and ads:** claims in ads must match the PDP and the label.

## 3. Environmental claims (US Green Guides, 16 CFR Part 260)
| Claim | Rule |
|---|---|
| General benefit ("eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable") | Nearly impossible to substantiate. Avoid it, or qualify it with a specific, substantiated benefit |
| Recyclable (§260.12) | Unqualified only if recycling facilities are available to a substantial majority (60%) of consumers where it's sold. Otherwise qualify it |
| Recycled content (§260.13) | Only material diverted from the waste stream counts. State the percentage if it isn't 100% |
| Degradable (§260.8) | Unqualified only if it fully decomposes within 1 year after customary disposal. Items that go to landfill or incineration can't claim it unqualified |
| Compostable (§260.7) | State if it needs an industrial facility and whether such facilities are widely available |
| Free-of (§260.9) | Deceptive if the substance was never associated with the category, or was replaced by something with similar risk |
| Carbon offsets / "carbon neutral" (§260.5) | Competent and reliable scientific and accounting methods. Disclose if the reductions won't happen for 2+ years. Don't claim reductions that are already legally required |

**Scope and qualifiers:**
- Say whether the claim covers the product, the package or one component.
- Qualifiers must be clear, prominent and next to the claim.
- Never present a company-level achievement as an attribute of the product.

**Aspirational claims** ("net zero by 2040") need a real plan and interim targets. Never present them as current-state attributes.

**Substantiation ladder:**
1. Third-party certified.
2. Independently verified LCA or audit.
3. Internal test with a documented method.
4. Internal calculation from industry data.
5. Asserted.

"Asserted" blocks the claim; "calculated" gets a warning.

**EU (consumer claims):** Directive (EU) 2024/825 has applied since 27 September 2026, through national law. It bans:
- generic environmental claims without recognized excellent environmental performance
- claims of neutral, reduced or positive impact based on offsetting
- sustainability labels that aren't based on certification schemes or set by public authorities

Check each market's transposing law; the Commission opened infringement proceedings against many member states for late transposition in 2026. The separate proposed Green Claims Directive stalled in June 2025 and has not been adopted. **Don't treat it as law.**

**UK:**
- Follow the CMA Green Claims Code.
- Under the DMCCA 2024, the CMA can impose fines directly for unfair consumer practices (since April 2025).

**Retailer programs:** never imply a badge or program status the SKU doesn't hold. For example, Amazon's Climate Pledge Friendly requires a qualifying certification.

## 4. EU / UK product-claim pointers (flag for local counsel)
- **EU food:**
  - Health claims only from the EU Register (Regulation 1924/2006; authorised list in 432/2012).
  - Food information under 1169/2011, including allergen emphasis.
- **EU cosmetics:** claims must meet the common criteria in Regulation 655/2013. Safety and labeling fall under 1223/2009.
- **EU product safety:** the General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR) applies to online listings. Required information includes the manufacturer or EU responsible person, product identifiers, and warnings.
- **UK:** retained rules diverge over time. The MHRA covers medicines and devices.
- **Other markets:**
  - Canada: bilingual (English/French) labeling; Natural Health Product rules.
  - Germany: HWG restricts health advertising.
  - Mexico: NOM-051 front-of-pack warnings.
  - Australia: TGA and FSANZ.

## 5. Reviews, UGC and safety signals
- **Moderation must be rating-neutral.** Criteria such as PII, profanity, off-topic or clearly false content are published and applied the same way to every review, whatever its rating. Suppressing reviews for negative sentiment is prohibited (FTC 16 CFR 465.7).
- **Safety and adverse-event mentions** in reviews, Q&A or social posts ("rash", "burn", "choking", "allergic reaction", "hospitalized") go to the brand's safety or regulatory team.
  - Never use them in copy, and never suggest removing them.
  - Supplements, OTC drugs and cosmetics (MoCRA) carry serious-adverse-event reporting duties: within 15 business days of receipt, plus follow-up information within 15 business days for one year.

## 6. Commercial terms with retailers (antitrust)
- **Robinson-Patman Act (US):** no price discrimination between competing retailers. Promotional allowances and services must be offered to competing customers on proportionally equal terms. Flag any differential term for legal review. Federal enforcement revived in 2024–25 and then wound down, so private suits are now the main exposure.
- **Never share or request competitor pricing or plans.**
- **Resale prices:** a brand may publish suggested retail prices and adopt a unilateral MAP or pricing policy. An *agreement* with a retailer on its resale price is judged under the rule of reason federally, but is per se illegal in some states. Never agree or negotiate resale prices; route to legal. MAP limits *advertised* prices only.
