# Launches, Announcements & Press Releases

## Launch tiering
Tier the launch before planning it. Effort should match the impact.

| Tier | What | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | New product, major platform change, new market | Full campaign: positioning, analyst and press briefings, customer comms, sales enablement, web, events, executive voice |
| 2 | Significant feature, notable partnership, major customer win (with permission) | Announcement post, customer email, sales enablement, social, possibly a press release |
| 3 | Incremental improvement | Release notes, in-product messaging, customer-success awareness |

Always define a **minimum viable launch** too: what must ship even if everything else slips.

## Launch plan essentials
- **Positioning** for the launch: who it's for, the problem, what's new, why it matters now, and proof. If proof is pending, use beta or early-customer evidence, with permission.
- **Readiness gates:**
  - the product is actually available as described
  - pricing and packaging are final
  - documentation is published
  - support and sales are trained, with FAQs and objection handling
  - legal has reviewed the claims
- **Sequencing:**
  1. Internal enablement first.
  2. Then customers who will be affected: they should never learn about a change from the press.
  3. Then analysts and press under embargo where relevant.
  4. Then the public.
- **Partner kit** where partners are involved: approved copy, assets, timing, and specific asks.
- **Post-launch review at 2–4 weeks:** goals vs. actual results, what worked, what didn't, feedback themes, and follow-on actions.

## Press releases
**Structure:**
1. Headline: [Company] [does what] to help [audience] [achieve outcome].
2. Subhead: the key differentiator or a verified fact.
3. Dateline and lead paragraph: who, what, when, where, why.
4. Why it matters now: market context, sourced.
5. Executive quote.
6. Product or announcement details.
7. Customer or partner quote, with written approval.
8. Availability and pricing, if public.
9. Boilerplate.
10. Media contact.

**Rules:**
- **Quotes must add insight a journalist could use:** a point of view, context, a specific implication. Delete "We're thrilled to announce…" quotes.
- **No unverifiable superlatives** ("first", "only", "leading") without substantiation. Journalists check, and a caught fabrication damages the relationship long-term.
- **Every customer and partner mention and quote needs written approval.** For public companies, coordinate with their communications or investor-relations team.
- **Public companies (us or the customer):** material announcements follow disclosure policy, with legal review and approved forward-looking-statement language.

## Media pitches
- Three sentences: what's new and why it's news, evidence or traction, and why this journalist (reference their actual coverage).
- Offer something useful: a briefing, data (real), a customer interview (approved), or an exclusive.
- No mass blasts. Personalize to each journalist's beat, or don't send.

## Newsjacking (reacting to news)
Score each opportunity 1–5 on:
- relevance to our expertise
- audience interest
- whether we have something unique to add
- brand safety
- timeliness

Proceed only when the score is high **and** the brand has standing to comment. Avoid tragedies, litigation, politics and anything market-moving.
