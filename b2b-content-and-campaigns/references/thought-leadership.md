# Executive Thought Leadership & LinkedIn

The goal is to be remembered by the few hundred people who matter, such as buyers, partners, analysts and candidates. Reach is not the goal. Credibility compounds; engagement bait erodes it.

## Foundations
- **Positioning line first:** "[Executive] helps [audience] understand/solve [problem] through [distinct perspective]." Without it, posting becomes random.
- **2–4 content pillars.** Each must pass four tests: genuine expertise, the audience cares, sustainable for 6+ months, and connected to the business strategy. Rough mix: mostly core pillars, some adjacent topics, a little personal.
- **Voice profile** built from real samples (see `positioning-and-messaging`, voice extraction). The executive's real views and stories only.
  - If no samples exist, draft in a plain, direct executive register.
  - Don't leave a hole. Draft 2–3 candidate takeaways built only from the facts, each labeled `[EXEC: keep only if you believe it]`, so the executive picks, rewrites or kills one. A candidate is never their view until they choose it.
  - Never invent their view.

## Post types that work for enterprise executives
| Type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Point of view | Clear claim → reasoning from experience → the nuance or exception → what to do instead |
| Lesson from experience | Situation → what was hard → what we did/learned → takeaway (real story, approved details only) |
| Framework | The problem → steps or model → one example → when it doesn't apply |
| Proof / customer outcome | The customer's challenge → what changed → number (approved, permission-checked) → the broader lesson |
| Market observation | A specific signal (sourced) → what it means → prediction with appropriate confidence |

## Writing rules
- **Open with the point.** The first line should make the target reader stop because it's relevant, not because it's sensational.
- **One idea per post.** Short paragraphs; plain words; no hashtag walls. Use one to three relevant hashtags if the executive uses them at all.
- **End with substance**, or with a genuine question the executive would actually want answered. Avoid "Agree?" and "Thoughts?".
- **Use digits and specifics:** real numbers, named (permitted) examples, concrete situations.
- **No engagement bait:**
  - "Comment X and I'll send you…" gates
  - manufactured controversy
  - "unpopular opinion" framing for popular opinions
  - humblebrags
  - "I'm humbled/thrilled to announce"
- **Check against `customer-facing-quality-gate/references/ai-tells.md`**, especially "not X, but Y" reframes, staccato fragments and moralizing closers.

## Sensitivity rules
- **Public-company executives.** No material non-public information. Nothing about financial performance or forecasts beyond what has been disclosed. No forward-looking statements without approved language. Route through communications or legal per company policy.
- **No confidential information.** No customer names, pricing, roadmap or internal metrics without approval.
- **Personal views disclaimer** where company policy requires it.
- **Approval.** The executive approves every word before posting. The agent never posts on their behalf without an explicit instruction for that specific post.

## Profile essentials for executives
Keep the title in the headline, since for senior leaders it is credibility. The About section covers who they help, real outcomes, their point of view, and how to engage. Name, company and focus must be consistent across the site, speaker bios and press.

## Measurement
Review monthly:
- which pillars drive conversations with target accounts (meetings, DMs from buyers, inbound mentions in deals)
- versus which drive only impressions

Judge against the executive's own history. Don't use generic benchmarks.
