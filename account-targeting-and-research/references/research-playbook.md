# Account Research Playbook

## Source priority
When sources conflict, trust them in this order:
1. **The company's own filings and statements.** Annual report or 10-K, 10-Q, proxy, earnings-call transcripts, investor-day decks, press releases, and the company website for self-described facts.
2. **Regulators and official registries.** SEC/EDGAR, Companies House, procurement portals, and public-sector budget documents.
3. **Executives' own words.** Interviews, conference talks, podcasts and LinkedIn posts by named leaders.
4. **Reputable business press.**
5. **Data providers** such as Crunchbase, PitchBook or ZoomInfo. Treat their numbers as estimates.
6. **Job postings.** Strong signal of investment and tech stack, weak signal of timing.
7. **Review sites and communities.** G2, Gartner Peer Insights, Glassdoor and Reddit. A pattern counts as a signal; a single post is noise.
8. **General social media.**

Record the URL and access date for every non-trivial fact.

## Research dimensions
Go deep only where it changes what we do.

### 1. Business model and performance
- Segments, brands, channels and geographies, and which ones are growing or shrinking.
- Public companies: segment revenue, margin trends, guidance changes, cost programs.
- Retail and CPG: number of brands and SKUs, owned DTC vs. wholesale vs. marketplaces, private label, international footprint.
- Ask: "If I were their CFO, what keeps the number from being hit?"

### 2. Stated strategic priorities
- Quote the language leadership uses, such as "accelerate digital", "operating efficiency", "personalization at scale" or "AI".
- Use their phrasing in outreach and business cases. Buyers respond to their own words.
- Earnings-call Q&A shows what analysts push on. That is usually where the pressure is.

### 3. Leadership and org
- The executive team and leaders in the buying function, plus tenure. Leaders new to their role (roughly the first 6 months) often re-evaluate vendors. Treat this as a hypothesis to test, not a fact.
- Reporting lines where they can be found.
- Recent hires and departures.
- Re-check that each person is still in the role. People-data staleness is the most common embarrassing error.

### 4. Technology and current approach
- Systems in our area (e.g., PIM, DAM, CMS, commerce platform, CRM, martech, data platforms, AI tools), each with a confidence level:
  - **High:** stated by them, or required in a job post.
  - **Medium:** a site script or partner case study.
  - **Low:** an industry norm.
- How they solve the problem today: agencies, in-house teams, manual work, an incumbent vendor, or a homegrown build.

### 5. Signals and triggers
See the trigger table in SKILL.md. Date every signal.

### 6. Risk and sensitivity
Check for:
- layoffs
- litigation
- data breaches
- regulatory actions
- activist investors
- recent bad press
- open procurement
- quiet periods

These change tone and timing. Never reference them clumsily in outreach.

### 7. Existing relationship with us
- Past opportunities, current or former customers, open support issues, event attendance.
- Former users of our product now at the account (a strong champion path).
- Partners in common: SIs, agencies, technology partners.

### 8. Competitive situation
- Incumbent and alternatives, including do-nothing and building it themselves.
- Contract and renewal clues: announcement dates of the incumbent deal, typical term lengths.
- Signs of dissatisfaction, judged as a pattern rather than one-off complaints.

## Useful public sources by company type
- **Public companies:**
  - 10-K Item 1 (business) and Item 1A (risk factors, a frank list of worries).
  - MD&A.
  - Earnings transcripts.
  - Investor-day presentations.
  - Proxy statements (executive compensation metrics show what leaders are paid to move).
- **Private and PE-backed companies:** press releases, PE firm portfolio pages (value-creation themes), executive interviews, job posts, trade press.
- **Public sector:** budgets, strategic plans, procurement forecasts, published RFPs and awards, board or council minutes.
- **Retail and CPG specifically:**
  - Their own PDPs and marketplace listings (content quality, consistency, gaps).
  - Number of SKUs and brands.
  - Retail partners.
  - Seasonal calendar.
  - Product-launch cadence.

## Quality bar before handing off
- [ ] Every fact has a source and date. Inferences are labeled, with the reasoning shown.
- [ ] People's current roles are verified.
- [ ] Insights are non-obvious and paired with "so what".
- [ ] Unknowns are listed explicitly, ranked by how much they matter.
- [ ] Nothing in the brief came from behind a login or from personal-life data.
