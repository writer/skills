# Web & Landing Pages (B2B)

## Audit method
Classify the page type first (homepage, solution, industry, campaign landing page, comparison, pricing), because the job differs by type. Fetch the actual page and quote its copy exactly. Never audit from assumptions.

**The 5-second test:** can a first-time visitor in the target persona tell
- what this is,
- who it's for,
- why it's credible, and
- what to do next?

**Score each section 1–5, with evidence quoted from the page:**

| Section | Weight | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Above the fold | 25% | Outcome-focused headline for a specific audience; subhead adds the how; one primary CTA; credibility signal visible |
| Value proposition & differentiation | 20% | Differentiator explicit and defensible; benefits tied to features; passes "could a competitor say this?" |
| Proof | 15% | Named customers/logos (with permission), quantified outcomes, relevant to this audience; proof near decision points |
| Clarity & copy | 15% | Customer language, scannable, specific, no jargon or filler |
| Objection handling & trust | 15% | Security/compliance (exact), integration, implementation, pricing approach, FAQ for the top objections |
| Conversion path | 10% | CTA matches the visitor's stage (demo vs. assessment vs. content); low-friction forms; a secondary path for not-ready visitors |

**Score bands:** 4.5+ excellent · 3.5–4.4 good · 2.5–3.4 needs work · <2.5 rebuild.

**Deliver** prioritized fixes, each with:
- the current copy, quoted
- why it's weak
- a rewrite
- an alternative rewrite
- expected impact (high, medium or low; **never invent percentage lifts**)
- effort

Order the fixes by impact against effort.

## Headline rubric
- **1:** "Welcome to [Company]" or a slogan with no meaning.
- **3:** "[Feature]-powered [category]."
- **5:** "[Specific outcome] for [specific audience], without [specific obstacle]." Every word in it must be true and provable.

## Page structures

**Solution or industry page:**
1. Outcome headline.
2. The problem in the buyer's words.
3. How it works, in 3 steps.
4. Outcomes with proof.
5. Use cases or roles.
6. Integration and security.
7. Customer story.
8. FAQ.
9. CTA.

**Campaign landing page:**
- Matches the ad or email that sent the visitor: same promise, same language.
- One action.
- No competing navigation.
- Proof next to the form.

**Comparison page:** see `competitive-and-objections`. Include an honest "where they win" section.

## Conversion principles (B2B)
- **Make the CTA an offer, not a verb.** Say what the buyer gets, how fast, and what it costs them. "Send 25 SKUs, see the rewritten drafts" beats "Book a demo".
  - Use only offers the company actually provides.
  - Pair "Talk to sales" with a lower-commitment path.
  - Put the strongest proof and the risk reducer (pilot terms, security summary) right next to the CTA.
- **Forms:** ask only for what routing truly needs.
- **Give the champion something to share internally:** a one-page summary, a security overview, a business-case template.
- **No dark patterns.** No fake scarcity, no countdown timers on B2B offers, no pre-checked consent boxes.

## SEO and AI-search basics for B2B pages
- **One H1.** A unique title and meta description, written for the click. Roughly 50–60 and 150–160 characters is a display heuristic: Google truncates by pixel width and may rewrite them.
- **Match search intent.** Comparison queries get comparison content. "What is" queries get clear definitions.
- **Write for citation:**
  - put the answer first in each section
  - state facts plainly, with dates ("as of 2026")
  - keep entity names consistent across the site and profiles
  - make sure content is readable without JavaScript
- **Structured data:** only for content visible on the page, such as Organization and BreadcrumbList. Don't promise FAQ or HowTo rich results; Google no longer shows them (verify current status).
