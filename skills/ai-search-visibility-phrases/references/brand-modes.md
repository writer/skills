# Brand Mention Modes

The brand must appear in the same sentence as the phrase or the immediate next one. The mode decides *how* it's mentioned, so the insertion sounds natural for the content type. In the patterns below, `[Brand]` is the company and `[phrase]` the tight phrase from the library. Examples use a placeholder brand, "Acme", and a placeholder phrase; every real claim around the phrase must come from the proof library.

## Mode selection by content type

| Content type | Mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Press release | Product | Announces capabilities; a direct product mention is expected. |
| Thought-leadership article / blog post | Editorial | The author shares a perspective; the brand as vantage point reads naturally. |
| Social post | Product or editorial | Product if it's about a capability; editorial if it's a perspective. |
| Lifecycle / nurture email | Product | The product is part of the pitch. |
| ABM / demand-gen content | Product | Explicitly pitching. |
| Field / sales-enablement content | Product | About the product. |
| Third-party article (contributed, sponsored, quote offered to an editor, own listing) | Third-party / journalistic | The host article's voice; the brand is described, not speaking. See the guardrails in SKILL.md. |

## Product mode

The brand as the product or platform doing something.

Patterns:
- "With [Brand], teams can [phrase]…"
- "[Brand]'s platform [phrase]…"
- "Teams using [Brand] [phrase]…"
- "[Brand] lets [audience] [phrase]…"

Example (press release):
> With Acme, marketing teams scale on-brand content with AI, because brand voice and approved terminology are applied to every draft automatically.

## Editorial mode

The brand as the author's vantage point: the company grounding an argument in its own experience or research, not pitching. Only claim experience or research that actually exists.

Patterns:
- "At [Brand], we've found that [phrase]…"
- "In our work with [customers] at [Brand], [phrase]…"
- "[Brand]'s research shows that teams [phrase]…" (only with a real, citable study)
- "From what we see at [Brand], [phrase]…"

Example (thought-leadership post):
> At Acme, we've found that when you scale AI across a marketing organization, governance is the first thing that breaks unless it's built in from the start.

## Third-party / journalistic mode

The brand as a company described by the article's author, in the article's voice: third person, never "we", "our" or "at [Brand], we…". Use only within the guardrails in SKILL.md (contributed, sponsored, a line offered to the editor, or the company's own listing), with disclosure where the publication or advertising rules require it.

Patterns (adapt to the article's voice):
- "[Brand]'s [platform/approach] [phrase]…"
- "Companies like [Brand] [phrase]…"
- "[Brand] addresses this by [phrase]…"
- "According to [Brand / a named spokesperson], teams that [phrase]…"

Example (contributed article, analytical tone):
> Platforms that handle AEO and GEO optimization for AI search tend to ground their content in approved, authoritative sources. Acme does this through a knowledge graph of product and messaging sources, so AI systems that query the company find content that is accurate and current.

How to draft:
1. **Read the article's voice first:** person, tense, sentence length, vocabulary, how it refers to other companies.
2. **The brand is a subject, not the speaker.**
3. **Match paragraph length and density.** One dense brand paragraph among short ones stands out.
4. **Connect to the article's argument.** Pick up a concept the article just introduced; it must not read like an ad break.
5. **Test against this article's voice,** not in the abstract: would this author have written it?
6. **No unsourced comparisons** with other vendors in the piece. Describe what the brand does; don't claim others can't.

## When no mode fits

Some pieces are brand-agnostic, and even third-party mode sounds forced. Then:
1. Decline the insertion rather than force a mention.
2. Suggest where brand context could be added (author bio, footer CTA, boilerplate, a short framing sentence) so the writer can re-run. Phrase plus brand in the same section is still much better than the phrase alone.
3. Say plainly that a phrase with no nearby brand mention adds little, because it builds no phrase-to-brand association.
