# Healthcare & Life Sciences Marketing and Communications Rules

Load this for content from or for health systems, payers, life-sciences companies or health-tech vendors. Load it too for any patient-facing material and any healthcare claim. It is a risk checklist, not legal advice. Route anything flagged here to the client's compliance, privacy or medical-legal-regulatory (MLR) review. Items marked [VERIFY] may have changed.

## 1. HIPAA in marketing
- **Marketing** (45 CFR 164.501) is a communication that encourages buying or using a product or service.
  - It excludes treatment and operations communications about the covered entity's own services or treatment alternatives.
  - The exclusion is lost when a third party pays for the communication.
- **Using PHI for marketing requires the patient's written authorization** (164.508(a)(3)).
  - The only exceptions are face-to-face communications and promotional gifts of nominal value.
  - If a third party pays, the authorization must say so.
  - Selling PHI also requires authorization.
- **Fundraising** communications need a clear opt-out (164.514(f)).
- **Business associates** may use PHI only as their BAA allows. **Never use a client's PHI to market our own products.** That includes patient lists in ad audiences, look-alikes or CRM enrichment.
- **Case studies contain no PHI.** De-identify using either:
  - the Safe Harbor method (removing 18 identifiers), or
  - expert determination (164.514(b)).

  Beware small numbers and rare conditions, which can re-identify people.
- **Patient testimonials and stories** require the covered entity to hold a HIPAA authorization covering the name, photo, condition and the fact of being a patient. The proof library records that it exists; the agent never collects it.
- **Review replies:** never confirm someone is a patient. OCR has settled cases over review replies that disclosed PHI.
- **Tracking technology:**
  - No third-party pixels or ad tags on authenticated portal pages, or anywhere patients enter health information.
  - In 2024 a court vacated only the part of OCR's guidance covering unauthenticated pages; the rest of the guidance stands.
- **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule** (amended 2024): covers health apps outside HIPAA. Sharing health data with advertisers without authorization can count as a breach.
- **State consumer health data laws** (e.g., Washington's My Health My Data Act; Nevada, Connecticut):
  - Consent to collect, plus separate consent to share.
  - Washington bans geofencing around in-person health facilities.
- **Substance-use-disorder records** (42 CFR Part 2): never use them in marketing.

## 2. Health and medical claims
- **Substantiation:** health claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence, generally well-controlled human studies of the specific claim (FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance). A testimonial can't make a claim we couldn't substantiate directly.
- **"Cleared" vs. "approved":**
  - "FDA cleared" for 510(k) devices.
  - "De Novo authorized" for De Novo devices.
  - "FDA approved" only for PMA devices, approved drugs and licensed biologics (BLA). Humanitarian Device Exemption devices are "approved" under an HDE.
  - Registration or listing never means approval.
  - A non-regulated product isn't "FDA compliant".
- **Claims can make software a medical device.** Intended use is defined by marketing claims (21 CFR 801.4).
  - For AI or software, words like "diagnoses", "detects [disease]", "predicts [condition]" or "recommends treatment" can create device status, or take the product outside the non-device clinical-decision-support criteria. FDA revised its CDS guidance in January 2026; it now allows a single recommendation when only one option is clinically appropriate.
  - Have regulatory counsel map claims to the current guidance.
  - Prefer accurate language such as "supports clinician review", when true.
- **Drug and device promotion** (life-sciences clients):
  - On-label claims only.
  - Fair balance of benefit and risk information (21 CFR 202.1).
  - All content goes through the client's MLR / promotional review.
  - DTC ads follow FDA's major-statement rule (2024). FDA enforcement on DTC ads intensified from September 2025, and a rule to tighten broadcast-ad requirements has been proposed [VERIFY current status].
- **Endorsements** (FTC Guides, 16 CFR Part 255):
  - Disclose material connections, such as paid clinician advisors or investors.
  - Expert endorsers must have the expertise implied.
  - A "results not typical" note doesn't cure an atypical result.
- **Credentials:**
  - Never say "HIPAA certified" or "HIPAA compliant product".
  - Name HITRUST assessments exactly (e1, i1 or r2), with scope.
  - "Clinically validated" requires naming the study.
- **Awards and rankings** (e.g., US News, Leapfrog marks) generally need a license from the publisher [VERIFY per program].

## 3. Inducements and fraud-and-abuse
- **Patient incentives** for Medicare or Medicaid beneficiaries implicate the beneficiary-inducement civil monetary penalty. Examples include gift cards for screenings and free services.
  - OIG's nominal-value guidance is $15 per item and $75 per year in aggregate, and never cash or cash equivalents.
  - Route these to counsel.
- **Selling to providers:**
  - The Anti-Kickback Statute applies to anything of value tied to federally reimbursed referrals or purchases. That includes free tools, pilots bundled with reimbursable items, meals and travel.
  - Follow each system's vendor-gift policy, and government gift rules for VA, state and public hospitals.

## 4. Payer marketing (Medicare Advantage / Medicaid)
- **Medicare Advantage** (42 CFR 422.2260–2274):
  - Required materials are translated into any language spoken by 5% or more of the plan's service area.
  - 12-point font minimum.
  - Required disclaimers, plus the Section 1557 notice of availability of language assistance. CMS dropped its separate multi-language insert / notice requirement for CY2027 materials.
  - Superlatives must cite their source.
  - Plan compliance review before use.
- **Medicaid managed care** (42 CFR 438.10): taglines in prevalent languages and large print, plus font minimums [VERIFY current rule].
- **TCPA healthcare exemption** (calls and texts):
  - Covers only free, exigent healthcare messages.
  - Strict frequency and length limits.
  - **Any marketing or billing content removes the exemption.**

## 5. Patient-facing plain language and health literacy
- **Mostly best practice, not law,** for private marketing. Exceptions: the Plain Writing Act binds federal agencies, and many states set readability minimums for insurance forms [VERIFY per state].
- **Standard:**
  - About a 6th–8th grade level.
  - Check with the AHRQ PEMAT or the CDC Clear Communication Index, not just readability formulas, which misfire on medical terms.
  - Main message first, active voice, terms defined in context.
  - Numbers as natural frequencies ("3 out of 100").
  - Person-first language.
  - Never label an audience "low literacy".
- **Clinical review:** any simplified clinical content gets clinical review before publishing.

## 6. Accessibility
- **HHS Section 504 rule** (2024): recipients of HHS funding must make web, mobile apps and kiosks meet **WCAG 2.1 AA**.
  - 15+ employees: by 11 May 2027.
  - Smaller recipients: by 10 May 2028.
  - An HHS interim final rule (May 2026) extended both dates by one year; the standard is unchanged.
- **ADA Title II web rule** (DOJ 2024): public hospitals and state systems must meet WCAG 2.1 AA by 26 April 2027 (population 50,000+) or 26 April 2028 (smaller entities). These dates come from DOJ's April 2026 interim final rule.
- **Sales implications:**
  - Expect buyers to ask for an accessibility conformance report (VPAT).
  - Never claim "fully accessible" without an audit.
  - State the WCAG version and level.

## 7. Language access (Section 1557, 45 CFR Part 92)
- **Notice of availability** of free language assistance, in English and at least the 15 most common non-English languages in the relevant state(s), on specified communications.
  - General advertising isn't on that list.
  - Ask the client's compliance team which assets qualify.
- **Qualified translators:** machine translation of critical content must be reviewed by a qualified human translator.
- **AI decision-support tools:** from May 2025, covered entities must identify and mitigate discrimination risk in patient-care decision-support tools (§92.210). Expect this in AI vendor due diligence.
- **Enforcement posture:** federal language-access enforcement changed in 2025 [VERIFY]. The regulations still apply.

## 8. AI in patient communications
- **California AB 3030** (effective 2025): generative-AI patient clinical communications from providers need a disclaimer and a path to a human, unless a licensed provider reviewed them.
- **Other states** are adding similar rules. Examples: Texas SB 1188 (practitioner disclosure of AI use in diagnosis, from September 2025) and Illinois limits on AI in therapy [VERIFY by state].
- **Rule of thumb:**
  - AI-drafted patient-facing messages get human clinical review, or a disclosure.
  - They never pose as a clinician.
