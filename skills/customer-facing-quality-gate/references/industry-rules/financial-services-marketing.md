# Financial Services Marketing & Communications Rules

Load this for any content that a bank, broker-dealer, investment adviser, insurer or lender will publish or send, or that the agent drafts for one. It is a risk checklist, not legal advice. **All FS customer-facing content goes through the institution's own compliance approval.** The agent drafts; the institution's principal or compliance team approves. Items marked [VERIFY] are subject to change.

## 1. Securities: broker-dealers (FINRA Rule 2210)
**Categories:**
- **Correspondence:** sent to 25 or fewer retail investors within 30 days.
- **Retail communication:** sent to more than 25 retail investors within 30 days.
- **Institutional communication:** for institutional investors only, and not to be forwarded to retail.

**Approval:**
- **Retail communications:** need approval from a qualified registered principal before use, with limited exceptions. Some must also be filed with FINRA.
- **Correspondence:** supervised under written procedures.

**Content standards:**
- Fair and balanced, with a sound basis for evaluating the facts.
- No false, exaggerated, unwarranted, promissory or misleading statements.
- No omissions of material facts.
- Benefits are balanced with risks.

**Prohibited and restricted:**
- **Projections or predictions of performance** are generally prohibited. Limited exceptions include hypothetical illustrations of mathematical principles and investment analysis tools (Rule 2214). A pending FINRA proposal (SR-FINRA-2026-004) would allow projections and targeted returns under conditions. Until it is approved, apply the prohibition [VERIFY approval].
- Don't imply FINRA endorsement.
- Investment-company rankings must meet Rule 2212. Other rankings and awards fall under Rule 2210's general standards.

**Social media:**
- Static content counts as a retail communication.
- Interactive content is supervised like correspondence.

## 2. Securities: investment advisers (SEC Marketing Rule, Rule 206(4)-1)
**What counts as an advertisement:** communications to more than one person offering advisory services, plus compensated testimonials and endorsements. Most one-to-one communications are excluded, but hypothetical performance is not.

**The seven general prohibitions:**
1. Untrue statements of material fact, or misleading omissions.
2. Material facts the adviser can't substantiate on SEC demand.
3. Misleading implications or inferences.
4. Benefits discussed without fair and balanced treatment of material risks.
5. References to specific investment advice that aren't fair and balanced.
6. Performance results or periods included or excluded unfairly.
7. Otherwise materially misleading content.

**Testimonials and endorsements:**
- Clear and prominent disclosure of whether the person is a client, whether they were compensated, and any conflicts.
- Compensated promoters need a written agreement unless total compensation is $1,000 or less in the preceding 12 months (or the promoter is affiliated). Disqualification checks also apply.

**Performance:**
- Net performance alongside any gross figure, with equal prominence.
- 1-, 5- and 10-year periods (private funds excepted).
- Never imply that the SEC approved it.

**Hypothetical performance, including targets and projections:**
- Only with policies ensuring it's relevant to the audience's financial situation.
- In practice, never use it in mass retail marketing.

**Records:** retain advertisements for 5 years under Rule 204-2.

## 3. Personalized content can become a recommendation (Reg BI)
- **Who Reg BI covers:** broker-dealers recommending a security, investment strategy or account type to a retail customer. It has four obligations: Disclosure, Care, Conflict of Interest, Compliance.
- **The risk:** personalized "next best product" content for a retail investor can become a recommendation.
  - Route it to compliance as advice, not marketing.
  - Form CRS must be delivered at or before the first recommendation.
- **Investment advisers** owe their Advisers Act fiduciary duty instead.

## 4. US consumer banking and lending
**UDAAP** (Dodd-Frank §§1031/1036):

| Standard | What it means |
|---|---|
| Deceptive | A material representation or omission likely to mislead a reasonable consumer, judged by net impression. Fine print doesn't cure a misleading headline |
| Unfair | Substantial injury that consumers can't reasonably avoid and that benefits don't outweigh |
| Abusive | Materially interferes with understanding, or takes unreasonable advantage of consumers |

**Service providers can be liable too.** Federal CFPB enforcement is much reduced, but the law hasn't changed. State AGs and state UDAP laws are increasingly active.

**Reg Z (Truth in Lending) advertising:**
- **Closed-end credit (§1026.24):** triggering terms are:
  - the down payment
  - the number of payments or the repayment period
  - the payment amount
  - the finance charge

  Using any of them requires the down payment, the repayment terms and the "annual percentage rate" (spelled out), and whether the rate may increase.
- **Rates:** any rate stated must be expressed as an APR. A simple rate can't be more prominent than the APR.
- **Home-secured credit:** extra rules and prohibited practices, including misleading "fixed" claims and implied government endorsement.
- **Open-end credit (§1026.16):**
  - Promotional rates must say "introductory" or "intro" next to the rate, give the end date, and state the go-to APR.
  - Deferred-interest offers need "if paid in full" wording.

**Reg DD (Truth in Savings) advertising (§1030.8):**
- Don't say "free" or "no cost" if any maintenance or activity fee can apply.
- Express any rate of return as the "annual percentage yield". An interest rate may appear only alongside the APY, and no more prominently.
- **Stating an APY triggers disclosures:**
  - variable-rate status
  - the as-of date
  - the minimum to earn the APY or to open the account
  - that fees could reduce earnings
  - for time accounts, the term and early-withdrawal penalty

**Deposit insurance:**
- Deposit ads carry the official advertising statement ("Member FDIC") under 12 CFR 328.3. Don't misrepresent FDIC insurance. Amended digital-channel and ATM signage rules (§§328.4–328.5) require compliance by 17 April 2027.
- Nondeposit investment and insurance products sold through banks need: "Not FDIC insured · Not bank guaranteed · May lose value".

**Data use and offers:**
- **Prescreened ("pre-approved") credit offers:** require the FCRA opt-out notice.
- **Affiliate data used for marketing solicitations:** requires notice and opt-out (Reg V).
- **Sharing nonpublic personal information with non-affiliates:** requires a GLBA / Reg P privacy notice and opt-out, subject to the exceptions.

## 5. Fair lending in marketing
- **No discouraging statements.** Reg B §1002.4(b) prohibits statements that would discourage applicants on a prohibited basis. Prohibited bases include race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, receipt of public assistance, and exercising consumer-credit rights. A CFPB rule effective 21 July 2026 narrowed this to statements showing intent to discriminate, and that rule is under court challenge. State fair-lending laws and the Fair Housing Act are broader, so keep the conservative standard: no copy or imagery that signals a preference or limitation on a prohibited basis.
- **Fair Housing Act:** no ads indicating a preference or limitation. Use the Equal Housing Lender/Opportunity legend or logo where required.
- **Targeting:**
  - Never exclude by geography or demographics that track protected classes; that is a redlining risk.
  - Ad platforms restrict targeting for credit, housing and employment ads (special ad categories).
  - The CFPB's 2026 Reg B rule removes disparate-impact liability under ECOA (effective 21 July 2026; under court challenge). Fair Housing Act disparate impact, state laws and state AGs still apply, as do ad-platform special-category limits.

## 6. Insurance
- **US:** state law based on the NAIC Unfair Trade Practices Act and its advertising model regulations. Annuity sales follow best-interest suitability rules. Medicare products follow CMS marketing rules.
- **Always route** insurance content to the insurer's compliance team. Rules differ by state.

## 7. UK and EU
**UK financial promotions:**
- A promotion must be made or approved by an authorised person (FSMA s21). Firms need FCA permission to approve promotions for unauthorised firms.
- Promotions must be "fair, clear and not misleading" (COBS 4.2.1R and sector equivalents).
- **Consumer credit:** using rates or cost figures triggers a representative example. The representative APR is the rate at or below which at least 51% of agreements are expected to be made.
- **High-risk investments and cryptoassets:** risk warnings, cooling-off for first-time investors, and no incentives to invest.

**UK Consumer Duty (FCA Principle 12):**
- Firms must deliver good outcomes for retail customers. The four outcomes cover products and services, price and value, consumer understanding, and consumer support.
- **Consumer understanding:**
  - Communications must be tailored to the audience, including vulnerable customers, and tested where appropriate.
  - No sludge, fake urgency or dark patterns.

**EU:**
- **MiFID II (investment communications):** past performance must not be the most prominent feature, must cover 5 years in 12-month periods, and must carry a warning.
- **Consumer Credit Directive 2 (Directive 2023/2225, applies from 20 November 2026):** credit ads must carry a clear warning that borrowing money costs money. They must not suggest that credit improves the consumer's financial situation [VERIFY national wording].

## 8. Agent-specific rules for FS
- **Never claim regulatory approval of a product or vendor.** For example, don't say "SEC approved", "FINRA approved", "SR 26-2 / model-risk-guidance compliant" or "DORA certified".
- **Retain everything.** Every draft and sent message must be capturable by the institution's archiving: SEA Rule 17a-4 for broker-dealers, Rule 204-2 for advisers. GenAI-produced communications follow the same rules.
- **Write performance and market commentary carefully.** No predictions, no "historically recovers within X months", and no hypothetical returns in retail content.
- **Use approved wording only.** Disclosures and legends come from the institution's approved library, word for word.
